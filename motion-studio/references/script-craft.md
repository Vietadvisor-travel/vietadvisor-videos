# Script craft — structures, hooks, gags, endings

Pick ONE structure per video (write it in the treatment as `// structure:`). Never reuse the structure of
the previous video in `stories/`. The format (directing.md) sets the look; the structure sets the story.

## 1. Structures (choose by what the content *is*)
| structure | shape | best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Promise → proof** | bold claim · 3 proofs (each a different beat type) · twist proof · verdict | explainers, guides |
| **Wrong way → right way** | hero does it wrong (gag) · consequence · local shows the way · hero redoes it right | scams, etiquette, transport |
| **Countdown with a turn** | N…2 rising · #1 subverts the list ("#1 isn't a place, it's a time") | lists, food |
| **A day in** | dawn → noon → sunset → night, light changes each act, one moment per act | places, cities |
| **Journey** | start point · obstacle · helper · arrival moment · what it cost (real numbers) | routes, loops, trains |
| **Mystery** | question in the hook · 2 false answers · real answer at ~70 % · why it matters | history, myths |
| **Two worlds** | split A/B · alternate scenes · they meet at the payoff | versus, tourist vs local |
| **Postcard** | almost no talking; 4–6 moments; one line per moment; music carries it | brand films, festivals |
| **Letter** | narrated as advice to a friend coming next month ("Bring cash. Skip Tuesday.") | practical, warm |

Every structure: **turn every beat with *but / therefore***, re-hook at ~30 s and ~60 s, and put the
saveable fact in the last 10 s.

## 2. Hook bank (first 1–3 s — rewrite in your own words, never verbatim twice)
- Contradiction: "Vietnam's best noodle soup isn't phở."
- Number with tension: "350 km. 3 days. One road people underestimate."
- Wrong-belief: "You don't need a visa run. You need this form."
- Stakes: "Arrive after 2 pm and this lunch is gone."
- POV: "POV: your first Hà Nội street crossing." (with `# moment kind=crossing`)
- Price shock: "This bowl costs less than your bottle of water."
- Question the viewer can't answer: "Which month is Hạ Long actually clear?"
- Before/after: "June green. September gold. Same valley." (`kind=seasons`)
- Sensory cold open: no words for 1 s — sizzle, steam, then the line (`kind=steam`)
- Mid-action: start inside the downpour / on the train street, explain later.

## 3. Gag patterns (one per 20–30 s; payoff the running gag at the end)
1. **Expectation flip** — host promises calm, cut to chaos (`gag` + `cam=snap`).
2. **Rule of three** — two normal items, third absurd.
3. **Deadpan reaction** — the CU reaction is the joke (hold 1.5 s, no caption).
4. **Literal visual** — say "a mountain of herbs", show an actual mountain of herbs.
5. **Escalation** — each beat the hero carries more stuff / gets wetter / sweats more.
6. **Fourth wall** — host turns to camera: "He's going to do it anyway."
7. **Callback** — the opening mistake returns, now done right.
8. **Smash cut to silence** — loud scene → hard cut to quiet wide, one line.

## 4. Dialogue that sounds real
- People don't explain; they react. Max 8 words a line; let the second line answer a different question.
- Give the local the knowledge and the warmth (never the joke at their expense).
- End a dialogue on a *button*: a small laugh, an offer, a gesture ("Make a wish first.").

## 5. Emotion curve (plan it before the beats)
`curious → amused → surprised → awed (moment) → warm (dialogue) → sure (payoff)`. Put the visual peak
(a `# moment`) where the curve peaks, and a quiet beat right after it. Light follows the curve: day for
information, sunset for feeling, night for intimacy and celebration.

## 6. Endings
- **Saveable line + image** ("Go before two." over the bowl) → brand end frame (auto).
- **Circle back**: last shot mirrors the first (same place, new light; the hero now crosses calmly).
- **Open door**: one more question → "Full guide at vietadvisor.travel".
Never end on "Thanks for watching" or a generic superlative.
