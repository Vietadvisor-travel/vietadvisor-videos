# Vietadvisor Story & Comedy Devices: a working toolbox

Scope: storytelling and comedy craft for the Vietadvisor animated channel (vertical ≤ 3 min + 16:9 YouTube ≤ 10 min).
Built from creator interviews, trope breakdowns, transcripts of jokes that landed, and story rules from Pixar and South Park, then applied to Ep. 4 "How to cross the street in Vietnam".
Researched 2026-10-10. Where a number is a craft rule of thumb rather than a measured fact, it is marked **(rule of thumb)**.

---

## 0. The diagnosis in one paragraph

The visuals are 7/10. The scripts are 4/10 because they are **lists of correct information with a character standing next to them**, not stories.
Every Ep. 4 transition is "and then" (Rule 1, and then Rule 2, and then Rule 3…). Dave has no goal, so nothing is at stake. Cô Phở delivers wisdom but wants nothing.
The narrator describes what is already on screen ("Dave crosses. Slowly. Steadily.") instead of adding a second layer. And every episode since Ep. 2 uses the same dialogue engine: *Dave asks a naive question → Cô Phở answers with one smug line.*
The fix is not more jokes on top. It is **want + obstacle + escalation + payoff**, with jokes growing out of character, the way OverSimplified's do.

## 1. What the best creators actually do (research notes)

**OverSimplified (Stuart Webster)**
- History is told through a cast of loud, flawed personalities. Napoleon is defined by insecurity ("Well, I'm Napoleon and I hate all of you"). Nations become blobs with eyes and feelings. Characters move constantly (shifting arms, head turns), so body language is funny even when no joke is being made (Central Trend).
- Joke mechanisms that became memes (TV Tropes "Funny/Napoleonic Wars"):
  - literal escalation: "Congratulations, it's a general," then the whole army comes out
  - anticlimax: France's inner voice tries diplomacy, France says "I declare war"
  - understatement vs violence: "Nice sledgehammers!"
  - deadpan retort: "Kind of a weird thing to say to your wife, Napoleon."
  - the overly specific denial as a running gag: "definitely average height for the time"
  - anachronism: "Where am I lmao" written on a Waterloo painting
  - repetition with escalation: "I pooed my pants!" → "I POOED MY PANTS AGAIN!!!"
  - sincere tonal whiplash at the end: "You're doing alright, kid."
- Running gags that travel between episodes: "…which enraged his father, who punished him severely" (art reused across unrelated videos); "Dude. (beat) Not cool."; the "But OverSimplified, it was the Vikings" mock-correction (TV Tropes Running Gag). Characters even turn up inside the NordVPN reads.
- Structure: a cold-open skit, then narration for context, then dramatized two-handers between facts. The narrator voices everyone. Multi-part releases, 3–4 uploads a year, each one an event (async.com, Creator Handbook). He started under Bill Wurtz's influence and cut profanity to stay classroom-friendly (YouTube fandom wiki).

**Kurzgesagt (Philipp Dettmer)**
- Research first, a rough script, then two or three experts, revised until everyone signs off. The core tension is "a fine line between over-simplifying and making things overly-complicated." Tone was deliberately softened over the years; they want viewers to finish hopeful ("losing hope is itself a way of losing") (Forbes 2024).
- Short form forced a rethink that took about a year: start immediately, cut 3 min to about 1 min, design the loop (Forbes 2024).
- A five-stage arc: Hook (a huge, scary question) → Grounding → Crisis (scale widens until we feel helpless, music shifts) → Pivot (reframe as beautiful) → Release (small but comforted). Optimism has to be earned: "Unearned optimism feels fake." The viewer is cast as "you," the smart companion (Livecounts case study).
- Extended analogy: one scenario (table tennis) held for about two minutes to carry several ideas. The self-demonstrating thesis turns it back on the viewer: "Right now, it's predicting a way more complex thing: you." (Prismiq analysis).

**Bill Wurtz**
- One person does the music, animation and deadpan voice. Very fast: some topics are on screen for about one second, with short jingles cut between lines (Wikipedia). The voice swings from flat speech to cheerful chanting, and short, blunt, liftable lines spread as reaction GIFs: "How 'bout I do anyway?", "How about maybe you chill?" (Know Your Meme).
- Lesson: **rhythm is a joke engine.** A line of 3–6 words, a musical sting, the next line. Blunt paraphrase of a historical actor's motive, in modern speech.

**Sam O'Nella Academy**
- Stylistic suck: crude art makes the jokes feel unpolished and honest. The verbal backspace: "Wait, no, that's shingles." The overly prepared gag: a long build to an absurd payoff. A consistent cold-open greeting and a sign-off that gets altered for laughs. Mock-groan reactions to his own puns (TV Tropes).

**CGP Grey**
- Topics are framed as puzzles. Openings state the subject flatly ("This video is about the difference between…"). One analogy carries the whole essay: "You may think you don't have anything in common with a horse, but listen — you do." Scripts are rewritten for months, the jokes are quiet throwaway frames, and research depth is far beyond the runtime (yespress profile).

**Extra History / Simple History / Kings and Generals**
- Extra History and Simple History: under 10 min, a narrator plus simple figures, context before the main event. Kings and Generals builds stakes with maps, troop numbers and terrain, so "who will win" is visible before the clash. (I found no first-party write-up of their craft; this comes from viewing knowledge.)

**Pixar (Emma Coats' 22 rules), the ones that bite here**
- #1 We admire a character for trying more than for succeeding. #4 The story spine: "Once upon a time… Every day… One day… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally…"
- #6 Throw the polar opposite at your character. #12 Discount the first idea, and the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th.
- #13 "Give your characters opinions. Passive/malleable… is poison to the audience." #16 "What are the stakes? … Stack the odds against." #19 Coincidences can get characters into trouble, never out of it.

**South Park (Parker & Stone)**
- Between every pair of beats, the word must be **"therefore" or "but," never "and then."** Otherwise you have "simply a list of events" (Writer Unboxed).

**Travel creators**
- Best Ever Food Review Show: a goofy, self-deprecating, respectful host, gentle verdicts ("that's not my favorite"), and a **local expert beside him**. The stakes are the challenge food plus place and people, not shock (Roller Bag Goddess).
- Mark Wiens: the reaction face is the product ("the delicious food face helps others feel it"), and "the real reason… is the people" (Simon Stanley Q&A).
- Yes Theory: a mission brand ("seek discomfort"), the viewer as companion, wide/personal shot contrast, a juxtaposition reveal, next-episode tease (Prewrite).
- Short-form craft (NPR Training): a 3-second hook, one big idea, a problem or mystery up front, signposts ("here's the thing"), an arc from promise to resolution. Hook editing: pattern interrupts, a text-only beat to punctuate, cut dead lead-in seconds (Blackman).

**Comedy theory, so we know why a joke fails**
- Benign violation (McGraw & Warren 2010): we laugh when something breaks a norm *and* feels safe, both at once. It becomes benign through an alternative norm, weak commitment to the norm, or psychological distance.
- For Vietadvisor: Dave nearly dying is a violation; chibi style + "he's fine" makes it benign. Mocking a culture is a violation with no benign side for the people mocked, so it falls flat **and** loses that audience.
- Rule of three: two set the pattern, the third breaks it, and the third must be the funniest (Wikipedia, comic triple).

## 2. The toolbox: 46 named devices

Format: **What** / **Why it works** / **Timing** / **Vietnam example**. Timings are rules of thumb.

### A. Story engines (the anti-bland layer)

**1. Want + Clock.** Give the protagonist a concrete want and a deadline in the first 5 s. / Stakes turn information into suspense (Pixar #16). / State the want by 3 s, the clock by 6 s. / Dave wants the last bowl of phở across the street; Cô Phở closes at 6:00, and it is 5:57.

**2. "But / Therefore" chain.** Each beat causes or complicates the next. / Keeps the "list" feeling away. / Audit every transition in the script. / Dave runs, BUT the riders' guesses break, THEREFORE he freezes, BUT the freeze corks the street, THEREFORE Cô Phở shouts "Like a buffalo."

**3. Fail before rule.** Show the mistake first, then name the rule. / We learn from consequences; the fail is the joke and the lesson is the payoff. / Fail 3–6 s, rule card 1.5 s, explanation ≤ 2 lines. / A rule with no fail in front of it = cut it, or fold it into another rule.

**4. Story spine (Pixar #4).** Once upon a time / every day / one day / because of that ×2 / until finally. / Forces causality. / Use it as a one-paragraph test before drafting. / "Every day Cô Phở sells 200 bowls. One day a tourist tries to cross for the last one…"

**5. The open loop.** Ask a question and delay the answer. / The Zeigarnik itch keeps people watching. / Open by 5 s, close at 70–90 % of runtime. / "There's one vehicle in Hà Nội that never gives way. We'll get to it. Dave won't."

**6. Mid-point reversal.** Halfway through, something we believed flips. / Resets attention. / At ~50 % of the YouTube cut. / Dave thinks the honking is rage; reveal: it means "I see you." Everything he did so far was wrong for the opposite reason.

**7. Earned optimism (Kurzgesagt pivot).** Let fear peak, then reframe it as beautiful or freeing. / The release feels earned because the dread was real. / Peak at ~75 %, pivot in one sentence. / "Seven million strangers, every day, agreeing without a word not to hit each other. That isn't chaos. That's Hà Nội being polite at 40 km/h."

**8. The extended analogy.** One image carries every idea (Kurzgesagt's table tennis, Grey's horse). / A single frame of reference keeps it from being a list. / Set it up once, return to it 3+ times. / Traffic = river, you = rock, buffalo = master. Every rule is a variation on "be the buffalo." Don't mix it with "math" and "dance" too.

**9. Cliffhanger / next-episode hook.** End on an unanswered, specific question. / Drives binge and subscribe. / The final 5–8 s. / "Next time: how Vietnam beat the biggest empire on earth… with sticks. In a river. At low tide." (Bạch Đằng, 1288.)

**10. Loop ending (vertical).** The last frame matches the first, so the video replays itself. / Rewatches feed the short-form algorithm (Kurzgesagt designs for the loop). / Final 1 s. / The last shot is the same wall of headlights; Dave, back at the kerb: "…Tomorrow." Cut to frame 1.

**11. "Go back and look" (planted background).** Plant a character or prop in the background from shot 1 and reveal it at the end. / Rewards rewatching; comments fill with "I saw her!" / Plant in 4+ shots, reveal in the last 15 s. / A tiny bà in a nón lá crosses slowly behind Dave the whole time. She gets the last bowl.

**12. Counterfactual "what if".** Briefly show the alternative history or choice. / Makes stakes concrete, and it's funny. / 3–8 s cutaway. / "What if Ngô Quyền had fought on open water instead? (cut: Han fleet laughing) He didn't." Or: "Dave considers waiting until 2028, when petrol bikes leave Ring Road 1."

### B. Character comedy

**13. Want-vs-flaw character.** Each character has one want and one flaw that sabotages it. / Jokes write themselves out of the gap. / Establish both in the first scene of a season. / Dave wants to be a "traveller, not a tourist"; his flaw is over-preparing from the internet and panicking when reality differs.

**14. Deadpan narrator vs panicking character.** Calm voice, frantic picture (OverSimplified, Wurtz). / The contrast is the joke, and it keeps the narrator credible. / Narrator line 0.3–0.5 s after the panic peaks. / Picture: Dave screaming, mid-street. Narrator: "Dave is doing great."

**15. Narrator-character argument.** A character talks back to the narrator, or corrects them. / Breaks the explainer monotony and gives the narrator a personality. / Once per episode, max twice. / Narrator: "Phở was invented in—" Cô Phở (to camera): "Guess." Narrator: "…I'll guess." Cô Phở: "Lower."

**16. Fourth-wall break.** A character acknowledges the viewer or the video. / Intimacy, surprise. / Use sparingly; the ending is the best slot. / Cô Phở to camera after Dave makes it: "You. Yes, you. You'd have run too."

**17. Catchphrase with variation.** A line repeated across episodes, altered each time (Sam O'Nella's sign-off). / Recognition is a reward; the variation is a surprise. / Same position every episode. / Dave before every disaster: "I read about this." Later: "I read about this… and I'm ignoring it."

**18. The overly specific denial.** A character denies something too precisely (Napoleon's "average height for the time"). / The defensiveness reveals the truth. / One line, then hold 1 s. / Cô Phở: "Southern phở is fine. For the South. In the South. Far from here."

**19. Straight man / comic.** One sane reaction anchors one absurd behaviour. / The audience needs a proxy to laugh with. / The reaction comes after the absurd beat, never before. / Minh (Grab driver) watches Dave bow to scooters, then quietly films it.

**20. Running character in the background.** A silent recurring figure in every episode (OverSimplified's Bucket). / Treasure hunt, fandom, merch. / Once per episode, never commented on. / The water buffalo appears somewhere in every episode: the Hội An bridge, the Hạ Long junk, the Bạch Đằng riverbank.

**21. Major-injury underreaction.** A character shrugs off catastrophe. / Benign violation: the stakes are huge, the reaction tiny. / A 0.8 s hold, then a deadpan line. / Dave's sandal is gone, his hat on a passing scooter's mirror. Dave: "…I'm fine. The hat had a good life."

**22. Sincere whiplash beat.** Once per episode, drop the irony for one honest line (Napoleon: "You're doing alright, kid"). / Makes the cast lovable, so viewers come back for them. / ~85–90 % of runtime, music drops out. / Cô Phở, quiet, as Dave sits down: "First time I crossed, I was six. My mother held my hand. Now eat."

**23. Personification of countries, empires, objects.** Nations and objects become characters with feelings (OverSimplified's blob nations). / Abstract geopolitics becomes a relationship comedy. / Introduce with a name card, then keep their voice consistent. / Đại Việt as a small, determined figure in a bamboo hat; the Yuan empire as a huge figure whose hat keeps growing with each conquest. The S-shaped map, sulking about being called "thin."

**24. The animal instigator.** An animal causes or solves the plot (OverSimplified's Pig War, Emu War). / Absurd scale contrast. / One per episode at most. / The buffalo crosses the Old Quarter calmly while seven million bikes flow around it, and that's the whole lesson.

### C. Joke mechanics (verbal)

**25. Rule of three.** Two set the pattern, the third breaks it. / Tension, then release. / Deliver the third fast; the break should be 2–4 words. / "To cross in Hà Nội you need patience, eye contact… and a will."

**26. Understatement.** Describe a big thing small. / The gap is funny, and it reads as confident. / A flat delivery. / Wide shot of the river of bikes. Narrator: "Traffic is a little busy."

**27. Overstatement / mock-epic.** Tell a small thing in epic register. / Bathos. / Use with cinematic music, then cut the music dead. / "And so Dave stepped off the kerb, as Ngô Quyền once stepped onto the Bạch Đằng… (music stops) …in flip-flops."

**28. Anachronism.** Modern words, objects or behaviour in a historical scene ("Where am I lmao", "Dude. Not cool."). / Instant relatability and incongruity. / As a punchline word, not a whole scene. / Trần Hưng Đạo checking a tide app: "Low tide 2 pm. Perfect." The Yuan admiral: "Who checks the tide?" "Us."

**29. Blunt motive paraphrase (Wurtz).** Say what a historical figure really meant, in five modern words. / Clarity is the joke. / A one-line sting with a music hit. / The Champa king, offered a princess for two provinces: "…Deal." (Ep 3 already did this, and it's the best line in the series so far.)

**30. Verbal backspace.** The narrator says something, then corrects it ("Wait, no, that's shingles"). / It feels live and fallible. / Correction within 1 s. / "Hà Nội has seven billion motorbikes— seven million. It feels like billions."

**31. Misdirection / bait-and-switch.** Set up an obvious ending, deliver a different one. / Surprise is the core of comedy. / The setup must be clear enough to predict. / "The secret to crossing is courage." (Dave puffs up.) "Just kidding. Courage gets you killed. It's boredom."

**32. Literal interpretation.** A character takes advice literally. / Character-driven, and visual. / Literal action 1 s after the advice. / Cô Phở: "Cross like a buffalo." Cut: Dave on all fours, mooing.

**33. Callback.** Reuse an earlier line or image in a new context. / Rewards attention and makes the episode feel designed. / Plant in the first third, pay off in the last. / Dave makes it across and says "Beep" to a rider, a callback to the horn chapter.

**34. The mock-correction from the audience.** Pre-empt the commenters ("But OverSimplified, it was the Vikings"). / Turns the comment section into part of the show. / Once per episode. / "Now some of you are typing, 'Actually, phở comes from Nam Định.' Cô Phở has read your comment. Cô Phở is from Nam Định."

**35. Tautological deadpan.** A plain statement of what the viewer can see, used *only* as a button after chaos. / Works once; overuse = bland. / After a 3+ s visual set piece. / "Dave ran." works once. "Dave crosses. Slowly. Steadily." does not: that's caption, not comedy.

### D. Joke mechanics (visual and editorial)

**36. Visual gag (silent).** A joke the picture tells with no VO. / It works in every one of the 8 languages: localization-proof. / 1.5–3 s, music sting. / A rider carrying 40 egg crates glides past the frozen Dave without a wobble. Dave looks down at his one water bottle.

**37. Cutaway gag.** Jump to an imagined or remote scene, then snap back (Family Guy, OverSimplified). / Pattern interrupt plus a joke. / 2–5 s, hard cut both ways. / "Dave's idea of Vietnamese traffic" (Mad Max war rigs) → snap back to a grandma on a bicycle with flowers.

**38. The "Meanwhile" cut.** Show what another character is doing at the same moment. / Dramatic irony and perspective. / One card ("MEANWHILE") plus 2–4 s. / Dave thinks the honking rider is furious. MEANWHILE, inside the rider's head: thinking about lunch.

**39. Funny background event.** A gag in the back of the frame while the foreground is serious. / Rewatch value, comments. / Never point at it. / While Cô Phở explains eye contact, a dog in the background crosses perfectly, making eye contact with every rider.

**40. Escalation ladder.** Each repetition is bigger (pooed → pooed AGAIN). / Rule-of-three at scene scale. / 3 steps; the 3rd is absurd. / Fail 1: Dave loses a flip-flop. Fail 2: his hat leaves on a scooter. Fail 3: he ends up on the wrong side of the street, holding someone's bánh mì.

**41. Smash cut / sound cut-out.** Cut from loud to silence, or epic to mundane, mid-word. / Shock laugh; a reset. / On the hardest consonant. / An epic orchestral build as Dave lifts his hand… total silence… one bicycle bell.

**42. Text-only punch.** Black frame, one line of type (proven in hook edits). / Forces attention; quotable as a screenshot. / 1–1.5 s. / Black: "HE DID NOT CROSS." → cut.

**43. Rhythmic cadence / jingle recap (Wurtz).** The recap becomes a 4-bar chant or musical sting. / An earworm; saves and shares. / 6–8 s at the end. / Sung over beats: "Slow — steady — don't run — don't stop — look — wave — let the bus go — get the phở."

**44. Freeze-frame label.** Stop motion, label everything like a field guide. / Fast information plus comic precision. / 2–3 s, 3–5 labels. / Freeze on the street: "RIDER (thinking about lunch)", "RIDER (fridge)", "BUS (doesn't care)", "DAVE (dying, emotionally)".

**45. Progress HUD as a joke.** An on-screen counter that the story keeps mocking. / Gives the vertical a visible goal, and the counter becomes a character. / Always on screen, updated after each fail. / "DISTANCE TO PHỞ: 12 m" → "12 m (STILL)" → "13 m??" → "0 m".

**46. Scale jump (Kurzgesagt).** Zoom from the personal to the cosmic, or the reverse, in one move. / Wonder and perspective. / 3–5 s camera move. / Dave's tiny step, crane up to the whole city, then the 73 million bikes of Vietnam as glowing rivers on a night map, then back down to his flip-flop.

### Joke density and comedy pass targets (rule of thumb)
- Vertical: a laugh beat (verbal or visual) every **8–10 s**; a "wow" or reveal every 20–30 s; no 10 s stretch of pure information.
- YouTube: a laugh every **15–20 s**, one set-piece gag per chapter, and one sincere beat per episode.
- **Punch-up pass:** for every joke, write 5 alternatives (Pixar #12), keep the one that comes from character, and cut any joke a viewer could predict from the first three words.
- **Silent test:** mute the VO. If the beat isn't funny or clear from the picture alone, add a visual gag (this also protects all 8 language versions).

## 3. Recurring cast proposal

Principles: every character has **a want, a flaw, an opinion, a catchphrase and a season arc** (Pixar #13). Jokes punch **up** (empires, colonial arrogance, bureaucracy), **sideways** (North vs South food pride, said by Vietnamese characters about themselves), or **at Dave** (the outsider as the butt, as in Best Ever Food Review Show's self-deprecation).
Ethnic-minority characters are always experts and hosts, never the joke. Revered national figures (and Hồ Chí Minh in particular) are portrayed respectfully; aim the comedy at invaders' hubris and at Dave.
**Continuity fix:** lock one voice per character. Cô Phở was voiced by `af_heart` in Ep 2 (the narrator's voice) and `af_bella` in Ep 3–4. Keep `af_heart` = narrator only.

**DAVE: the outsider (am_puck)**
- Want: to be "a traveller, not a tourist," i.e. to be accepted by Cô Phở. Flaw: confident from Google, falls apart on contact with reality. Secret strength: he tries, every time (Pixar #1), so viewers root for him.
- Catchphrases: "I read about this." (said before every disaster); "…Tomorrow." (when asked to do it again; already earned in Ep 4, keep it).
- Running gags: his laminated guide is wrong in a new way each episode; he keeps losing the same pair of flip-flops; his nickname "Buffalo" (from Ep 4) sticks.
- Arc: S1 learns one skill per episode, shown as a stamp in a passport-style "skill book" on the end card (collectable). S1 finale: Dave helps a *new* panicking tourist cross, and says Cô Phở's line, "Like a buffalo."
- Reactions: overcommits (bows, apologizes to scooters), then deflates. Never mocks the culture; he mocks himself.

**CÔ PHỞ: the Hà Nội gatekeeper (af_bella)**
- Want: to be proven right, and to sell out by 6 pm. Flaw: pride, mainly Hà Nội pride (phở Bắc above all), plus impatience. This lifts her above the "wise native" cliché: she's competitive and petty and loves to win.
- Catchphrases: "Guess." (from Ep 2–3, keep it), "Lower." (when anyone guesses a number), "Sit. Eat. Then talk."
- Running gags: she never reveals her real name ("Cô Phở is enough"); a feud with Chị Sáu in Sài Gòn; she reads the comments (via #34).
- Arc: history episodes open at her stall: she *is* the narrator's rival storyteller. She gets one sincere beat per episode (#22).
- Rule: she never explains a rule she can demonstrate. Show her doing it effortlessly; Dave tries, and fails.

**THE NARRATOR (af_heart)**
- Personality: deadpan, too honest, slightly on Dave's side, occasionally wrong and corrected by Cô Phở (#15). Never describes what's on screen; says what the picture can't (the counterpoint rule).
- Running gags: verbal backspace on numbers (#30); "Dave can't hear me. (beat) Dave, you can't hear me."; announces confident facts, then Cô Phở: "Lower."
- One meta line per season, max: "This is the part where I'd normally give you five rules. Dave has already broken four."

**BÀ (the silent grandma, no voice)**
- A tiny elderly woman in a nón lá who is always calmly competent: crosses during the whole street episode, eats the hottest chili in the food episode, out-hikes Dave in Hà Giang. She's the "Go back and look" figure (#11).
- Rule: she is the master, never the punchline. The joke is always Dave's reaction to her.

**MINH: Gen-Z Grab driver (am_adam or new voice)**
- Want: five stars and to go viral. Flaw: films everything, can't resist a prank. The straight man (#19) who deflates both Dave and Cô Phở, and the "young Vietnam" voice (cafés, slang, phones), so the cast isn't only tradition.
- Catchphrase: "Five stars, okay?" Arc: secretly practising his English with Dave; starts a TikTok called "Tourists vs Hà Nội," and Dave is the star.

**CHỊ SÁU: the Sài Gòn rival (new voice)**
- Runs a cơm tấm cart in District 1. Loud, fast, generous, adds sugar and herbs to everything. She opens every southern episode and roasts Cô Phở across the country by phone.
- The feud is sideways, affectionate North–South banter. Both women win sometimes. Payoff: one episode where they team up against Dave's ketchup.

**CHỊ MỶ: Hà Giang guide (expert host)**
- A Hmong guide and rider on the Hà Giang loop: the most skilled person in any episode she's in. She runs her own homestay and tours, and has the best sense of humour in the cast. Her culture is presented by her, accurately and never as a costume gag. The jokes come from Dave's fear of switchbacks.
- Catchphrase: "Slower is faster." (echoes the buffalo). Consult a real Hmong guide on name, dress and language before production.

**TRÂU: the water buffalo (mascot, silent)**
- Embodies "slow, steady, never panics." Appears once in every episode, uncommented (#20). End-card merch, sticker packs, the channel avatar.

**HISTORY ENSEMBLE (personified, OverSimplified-style)**
- Đại Việt: a small, stubborn figure in a bamboo hat with a sword. Underdog, clever, patient, always studying the terrain.
- Empires (Han, Song, Yuan, Ming): one oversized "Big Empire" whose hat grows with each conquest and shrinks after each defeat. Portray historical dynasties, never modern China or Chinese people.
- Monsieur Lacroix (already in Ep 2): colonial-era Frenchman, polite, pompous, always about to be outsmarted by a Hà Nội café owner. Comedy targets colonial arrogance, not French people.

## 4. Ep. 4 critique: brutally honest

**Vertical cut (`ep4-cross-street.story`)**

| Axis | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 5/10 | The headlight wall plus "7,000,000" is strong visually, but the VO asks a search query ("How do you cross…?"), not a story question. No character in danger, nothing at stake by 1.5 s. |
| Stakes | 2/10 | "Dave needs to get to the other side." Why? No want, no clock, no cost of failing. Pixar #16 fails. |
| Humour | 3/10 | About 3 real laugh beats in ~80 s ("Like a buffalo," "Dave ran.," "…Tomorrow.") ≈ 1 per 25 s, a third of the target. Rules 2–5 have no fail shown, so the plan's "fail before every rule" isn't executed. |
| Surprise | 3/10 | The horn reveal, the only counter-intuitive fact, is buried mid-rule-4 in half a line. Everything else is what a viewer expects. |
| Characters | 3/10 | Dave = "scared," Cô Phở = "smug" (the emotion tags *are* the characterization). Same naive-question → smug-one-liner engine as Ep 2 and Ep 3. Neither wants anything. |
| Pacing | 5/10 | Cuts are lively, but the rhythm is monotone: "Rule N. Statement. Explanation." ×5. The Hoàn Kiếm EV beat is a tangent that stops the story 15 s before the payoff. |
| Payoff | 5/10 | "Now cross back." / "…Tomorrow." is a good button, but there's no setup for why he'd need to come back. The buffalo set-up never pays off in the vertical. |
| **Overall story** | **3.7/10** | Correct, safe, forgettable. |

**YouTube cut (`ep4-cross-street-yt.story`)**: same core problems, plus:
- 7 chapters in ~4 min = a chapter card every ~35 s, each read aloud ("Chapter two. The river."). That's a speed bump repeated seven times. Use silent art-directed chapter titles; YouTube chapter markers do the navigation.
- The promise ("In the next four minutes… the sound that isn't rude… the one vehicle that never gives way") is the best thing in the script. But the "vehicle that never gives way" is answered in chapter 3 of 7, which closes the open loop far too early. Save buses for the climax.
- Chapter 6 "Easier ways" (Grab, xe ôm, buggy) comes right before the payoff and deflates it: "or just don't." Move it to a 10 s Cô Phở aside after a fail ("Or take a Grab. Coward.").
- The horn chapter is a 3-second "Beep. Beep beep. Beeeep." plus two lines. It's the mid-point reversal (#6) and should be a 60–75 s set piece.
- Good instincts to keep: the buffalo as the central analogy, "The buffalo learns," the river thesis, the Bạch Đằng tease.

**Root causes (both cuts)**
1. A list, not a story: every transition is "and then."
2. The narrator captions the picture ("Dave crosses. Slowly. Steadily. Alive.") instead of counterpointing it.
3. The fails aren't escalating or specific: no lost flip-flop, no consequence, no ladder.
4. No planted payoff: nothing set up in the first 20 s comes back in the last 20 s, except the buffalo, and only in the YouTube cut.
5. Safe tone: no character has an opinion strong enough to be wrong.

## 7. Episode writing checklist (use on every script)

1. **Logline test:** "[Character] wants [specific thing] by [clock], but [obstacle]." No logline, no script.
2. **Spine test:** fill Pixar #4 in one paragraph.
3. **Transition audit:** write "but" or "therefore" between every pair of `#` beats. Any "and then" gets rewritten or cut.
4. **Want/flaw check:** each speaking character wants something *in this scene*.
5. **Fail-before-rule:** every rule has a visible, escalating, specific fail in front of it.
6. **Density pass:** mark every laugh beat with a timestamp. Vertical ≥ 1 per 10 s; YouTube ≥ 1 per 20 s.
7. **Narrator pass:** delete any VO line that only describes the picture, unless it's a deliberate deadpan button (#35, once per episode).
8. **Plant/payoff map:** list 3 plants in the first third and where each pays off.
9. **Silent test:** watch muted. Every chapter needs at least one visual gag that survives translation into all 8 languages.
10. **Sincere beat:** one honest line near the end (#22).
11. **Punch-up:** 5 alternatives for each joke; keep the character-driven one.
12. **Guardrails:** no ethnic-minority or regional joke from an outsider; no jokes at victims of war; respectful portrayal of national heroes; comedy aimed at empires' hubris, bureaucracy, and Dave.
13. **Ending:** a callback button + loop frame (vertical) or a specific next-episode question (YouTube).
14. **Dialogue engine variety:** never two episodes in a row with "Dave asks → Cô Phở smug answer." Rotate in narrator vs Cô Phở, Minh vs Dave, Cô Phở vs Chị Sáu, silent visual scenes.

Stakes are planted in the river while the admiral laughs (anachronism + mock-epic). The tide goes out: the hat shrinks. Sincere beat: the stakes are still in museums today.
