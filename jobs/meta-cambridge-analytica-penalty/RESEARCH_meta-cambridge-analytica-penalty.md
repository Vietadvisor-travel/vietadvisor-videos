## Story spine (six beats)

1. 2014 to 2015: a personality quiz app takes data from 270,000 downloaders and their friends, up to 87 million profiles, and passes it to Cambridge Analytica.
2. 2015: Facebook learns of the transfer, demands deletion, accepts paper certifications, verifies nothing, and tells no users.
3. March 2018: a whistleblower and two newsrooms publish; the CEO stays silent for five days, then apologizes to Congress.
4. May 2018: Cambridge Analytica shuts down. The firm dies. Facebook does not.
5. 2019 to 2024: the fines stack up, $5B FTC, £500k ICO, $725M class action, $1.4B Texas, €1.2B GDPR, and the company keeps growing into a $200B revenue empire.
6. October 2026: the state that refused to settle gets a jury to count 43,899,725 violations and a judge to order $25B, and Meta appeals, so the biggest number in the story is not final.

## A. Topic framing

The slot thesis survives testing in its core, with three mandatory corrections:

1. Meta has not fallen. Q3 2026 revenue USD 64.60B, up 26 percent year on year; Family daily active people 3.64B, up 3 percent; FY2025 revenue USD 200.97B. No scale metric points down.
2. Correction one: the $25B is NOT final and NOT paid. Meta announced an appeal, the injunction is reserved, and Meta's Q3 10-Q records an accrual of an undisclosed amount for the penalty. The script must say "ordered" and "appealed", never "paid".
3. Correction two: the case is NOT only Cambridge Analytica. The 26 misleading statements covered data privacy, hate speech, misinformation, and content moderation. The reported Cambridge Analytica attribution inside the $25B (about $459M) rests on one outlet and collides with the separate multistate settlement's $459.29M Cambridge Analytica amount, so it must not be stated as confirmed.
4. Correction three: "standing" needs a second clause. Q3 2026 free cash flow was negative USD 2.12B and S and P moved the outlook to negative on 30 Oct 2026 over AI spending. The honest frame is "standing, spending, and still paying for 2014".
5. The week file's Meta Q3 date (29 Oct) is WRONG. The earnings release is dated 21 Oct 2026, the same day as the judgment. That same-day collision is the cold open.

Recommended thesis for the script, rewritten from evidence: "Meta did not fall when its data scandal broke, because the scandal was the business model working as designed. Eight years later a single state made it count the cost statement by statement, and the bill came to $25 billion, a bill Meta has not paid and is still fighting."

## B. Candidate angles and hooks

Angle 1: The arithmetic trial. How 26 statements times a state audience became 43,899,725 violations and $219.5B on paper. The trial is the story: a counting method, not a body count.
Angle 2: The state that said no. Every other participating state settled; New Mexico took the case to a jury and got a judgment many times the settlement share. A holdout story.
Angle 3: The price, not the punishment. $5B, $725M, $1.4B, €1.2B, $25B: a ladder of fines the company climbs while growing. The fines are a tax on the model, and the model survives each one.

Hook 1: "On the same day Meta reported $64.6 billion in quarterly revenue, a judge in Santa Fe ordered it to pay $25 billion. The company has not paid a cent of it."
Hook 2: "270,000 people took a personality quiz. 87 million people paid for it with their data. One state just finished counting what that was worth."
Hook 3: "The firm that harvested the data died in six weeks. The platform that gave it away is worth about $1.5 trillion. This is the story of the company that survived its own scandal."

## H. The 3 decision moments

Moment 1: 2015, Menlo Park. Facebook learns Kogan passed the data to Cambridge Analytica and accepts deletion certifications without verification and without telling users. The three-year silence is the original decision of the whole timeline; every later penalty traces to it.
Moment 2: 2021 to 2026, Santa Fe. New Mexico files, then refuses the August 2026 multistate settlement that the other participating states took, and bets on a jury. The bet returns a USD 25B judgment against a reported settlement share of roughly USD 349M.
Moment 3: 21 Oct 2026, Santa Fe. Judge Mathew calculates USD 219.5B under the statute and cuts it to USD 25B. The reduction is the decision that makes the number survivable on appeal and credible on screen: the law allowed nearly ten times more, and the judge still chose USD 25B.

## I. Mechanism

Platform design turns one quiz into 87 million profiles, because the permissions of the day let an app read the friends of every downloader. Ad targeting turns profiles into money, which is why the data was worth buying. Public trust statements turn into a liability surface: each misleading statement times its audience becomes a violation under a state consumer law with USD 5,000 per violation. A jury's arithmetic turns a trust deficit into a USD 219.5B ceiling. A judge's discretion turns the ceiling into USD 25B. An appeal turns the order into a pending number, not a payment.

## J. Counterfactual

If Facebook had verified deletion in 2015 and told users, the 2018 revelation would have been a closed incident, not a scandal; the FTC case, the ICO fine, and the New Mexico case all trace to the 2015 silence. If New Mexico had joined the multistate settlement, it would have received a reported share of roughly USD 349M instead of a USD 25B judgment it must now defend on appeal. If Mathew had imposed the full USD 219.5B, the appeal fight would be about constitutionality first and facts second; the USD 25B figure keeps the facts in play.

## K. Comparisons

## L. Myths and corrections

MYTH 1: "Hack" or "breach". FALSE as a technical claim. Nothing was broken into; the access was permitted by the platform's own permissions of the day. Facebook deputy general counsel Paul Grewal called the breach claim "completely false". Use "harvested through a permitted permission", not "hacked".
MYTH 2: "43 million victims". FALSE as a headcount. Violations equal statements times potential audience. The jury did not find 43 million individually harmed people.
MYTH 3: "USD 25 billion to users". FALSE. The penalty runs to the State of New Mexico. The order creates no claims process and no user fund.
MYTH 4: "USD 25 billion paid". FALSE. The appeal is announced, the injunction is reserved, and the 10-Q accrual is undisclosed. Say "ordered", never "paid".
MYTH 5: "Cambridge Analytica decided the 2016 election". UNPROVEN. Cambridge Analytica claimed credit and denied using Kogan data in the 2016 campaign; effectiveness is contested. Present claims as claims.
MYTH 6: "Facebook sold the data". CONTESTED. Meta's position is that it does not sell user data. The New Mexico case is about misleading statements, not a sale. Present both.
MYTH 7: "The ICO fine was huge". FALSE in scale. GBP 500,000 was the legal maximum under the 1998 Act, about 17 minutes of quarterly profit per The Register.
MYTH 8: "Meta is collapsing". FALSE on scale metrics. Revenue up 26 percent in Q3 2026, Family daily active people 3.64B. The pressure is cash flow and capital expenditure, not user or revenue collapse.
MYTH 9: "The New Mexico case is only about Cambridge Analytica". FALSE in scope. The 26 statements covered hate speech, misinformation, and moderation too, and the Cambridge Analytica attribution inside the USD 25B is single-source.
MYTH 10: "The FTC USD 5B punished Cambridge Analytica". FALSE in target. It punished Facebook for violating the 2012 order. Cambridge Analytica got a separate FTC complaint; Nix and Kogan got settlements.

## M. Takeaways

1. The scandal was not a bug in the model; the model was the scandal's delivery system. Friends permissions, ad targeting, and trust statements are one machine.
2. Paper beats outrage. The 2015 decision to accept certifications without verification created every later penalty.
3. A state that refuses to settle can rewrite the price. New Mexico's jury gamble is the only reason the number has two digits of billions.
4. Record penalties and record revenues now coexist. The film's tension is that both numbers are true.

## FIGURE TRAPS (read before scripting any number)

TRAP 1, 87M vs 65M vs 50M vs 57M: 87M is Facebook's April 2018 estimate; 65M is the FTC complaint's friends scope; 50M was first reporting; about 57M appears in the SEC settlement context. Never present one as "the" count without its source.
TRAP 2, 43,899,725 vs 43,899,720: the total includes 5 unconscionable-practice violations; the USD 5,000 multiplication uses 43,899,720. 43,899,720 times USD 5,000 equals USD 219,498,600,000. Multiplying the total instead gives USD 219,498,625,000, which is USD 25,000 too high.
TRAP 3, USD 219.5B vs USD 25B: USD 219.5B is the statutory ceiling the judge calculated, NOT the order. The order is USD 25B. Never call USD 219.5B the fine.
TRAP 4, TWO USD 459M FIGURES: the reported Cambridge Analytica share inside the USD 25B (single source) and the multistate settlement's USD 459.29M Cambridge Analytica amount are different numbers that happen to match. Do not merge them.
TRAP 5, TWO NEW MEXICO CASES: privacy case USD 25B (2021) versus child-safety case USD 375M plus USD 567M (2023). Never add them into one total.
TRAP 6, MULTISTATE TOTALS: teen settlement totals vary (USD 17.1B to USD 18.59B) across sources. The stable figure is the USD 459.29M Cambridge Analytica component. Use the component and range the total.
TRAP 7, Q3 2026 NET INCOME UP 264 PERCENT: the year-ago base of USD 2.71B was unusually low, so the percentage flatters. The cleaner operating line is operating income down 37 percent to USD 12.99B on the USD 9.89B legal charge. Do not present plus 264 percent as operating strength.
TRAP 8, S AND P FREE OPERATING CASH FLOW YEARS: Reuters and secondary accounts disagree on 2026 (USD 39B versus USD 22B deficit) and on 2028. Only the 2027 deficit (USD 71B) is consistent. Do not quote a full S and P path without attribution.
TRAP 9, FTC USD 5B TARGET: the USD 5B punished Facebook for violating the 2012 order, not Cambridge Analytica. Cambridge Analytica's own FTC treatment was a complaint plus Nix and Kogan settlements.
TRAP 10, "LARGEST" CLAIMS: USD 25B is among the largest state verdicts and penalties in US history (Reuters), not the largest fine ever in any category. USD 5B was the largest privacy penalty in 2019. Date every "largest" claim.
TRAP 11, JUDGE NAME SPELLING: the filing caption spells the judge "Mathew". Some coverage writes "Matthew". Use Mathew on screen.
