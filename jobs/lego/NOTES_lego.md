## Corrections that must survive into the script (from RESEARCH_lego.md)

1. PARTS COUNT TRAP. "More than 12,000 parts" is a FLOOR, not a precise
   number: sources count four different things (components about 13,000 down
   to about 7,000; SKU 12,500 down to 6,500; colours about 100 down to about
   50; suppliers about 11,000). NEVER write "12,000 down to 6,000" (that mixes
   components with SKU). Safe sentence: "Designers had gone from about 6,000
   distinct parts to more than 12,000. Knudstorp cut the usable library back
   to about 7,000."
2. NO BANKRUPTCY. LEGO did not file for bankruptcy in 2004. It was a
   liquidity and solvency warning stopped by a FORMAL KIRKBI loan of DKK 800M
   at CIBOR + 3%, maturing Dec 2011, with a 40% solidity covenant. Not free
   family money.
3. NOT THE FIRST NON-FAMILY CEO. Vagn Holck Andersen (1973-1979) was first.
   Knudstorp was second, a 3-year insider who wrote the "burning platform"
   diagnosis himself in June 2003 before taking the CEO job in Oct 2004 at
   age 35 (exact October appointment date still unpinned).
4. REVENUE DID NOT FALL 40%. Audited DKK chain: minus 28.9% in 2003 and minus
   6.8% in 2004, about one third total. 40% is Knudstorp's rounded way of
   speaking.
5. LEGOLAND SALE. Decided and classified as discontinued in 2004, but
   ANNOUNCED 13 Jul 2005 at EUR 375M, and LEGO kept 15% plus KIRKBI 15%. Not
   a clean full sale.
6. 2005 PROFIT, THREE FIGURES BY SCOPE. Net DKK 505M; pre-tax including
   discontinued DKK 702M; pre-tax continuing DKK 456M; a later restated table
   gives DKK 214M. Never blend the chains.
7. NOT THE WORLD'S LARGEST TOY COMPANY FROM 2014. Its own CFO said 2014 was
   still number two behind Mattel ($4.35B vs $6.0B). The crown is real in
   2024: about $10.85B, more than Mattel ($5.38B) and Hasbro ($4.14B)
   combined, but those two figures must be upgraded to 10-K filings before
   any hero graphic.
8. SOLD BELOW COST, SYSTEM LEVEL ONLY. True at system level (micro-motor
   and fibre-optic sets whose sourcing cost exceeded the whole box's retail
   price; the company had no per-product costing system), but no priced
   costing table for a specific set has been found. FORBIDDEN: stating a
   price for set 8456 or any specific set.
9. VERIFIED MILESTONES. Net loss 2003 DKK 935M, 2004 DKK 1,931M; 2004 cost
   cuts DKK 1,523M (20%) and about 1,000 jobs (the "3,500 jobs" figure is
   UNVERIFIED, it blends several rounds); net profit 2005 DKK 505M, 2006
   DKK 1,430M; revenue 2010 DKK 16,014M, 2015 DKK 35,780M, 2020 DKK 43.7B,
   2024 DKK 74,325M, 2025 DKK 83,530M (11.8x vs 2005); H1 2026 revenue DKK
   41.9B with consumer sales up 22% (NEVER annualise); The LEGO Movie 2014
   about $468.1M worldwide on a $60-65M budget; the sequel only about
   $190-200M on a $99M budget (gross DISPUTED between $192.3M and $199.6M);
   the 2017 crisis year still netted DKK 7.8B profit despite 1,400 job cuts;
   in the worst year 2004 the core toy business before write-downs still
   made DKK 103M (midpoint turn: the rescue was accounting cleanup plus
   cost cuts before it was growth); Morgan Stanley proposed selling the
   company for $1.7B in Nov 2004 (single source, IE case; hook candidate);
   the 1958 brick still interlocks with today's bricks (hook candidate).
10. OPEN RISKS BEFORE SCRIPTING. No audited interest-bearing debt figure for
    2003-2004 yet (the USD 800M figure is a June 2003 forecast warning, not
    a balance-sheet line); no exact October 2004 Knudstorp appointment date;
    no Robertson/Breen book pages for the 12,900 figure, the 13.5% ROS
    target, or the $1.6B economic value destroyed; no original Stafford
    interview with prices; no contemporaneous wire story for the LEGOLAND
    deal; 1998 annual report unread (1998 loss figure conflict: DKK 194M vs
    GBP 23M vs USD 27-48M). Living people (Knudstorp, Kjeld, Plougmann,
    Ovesen, Kjaerulff) may only be written from documented records, no
    inferred accusations.
